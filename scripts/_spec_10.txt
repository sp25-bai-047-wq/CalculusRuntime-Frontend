- `PS_D_CENTER_QUIZ` — Measures of center: computing mean, median and mode from small concrete data sets, effect of an outlier, effect of adding a constant or multiplying by a constant, weighted means, combining two group means, when the median beats the mean, skew and the relative position of mean vs median.
- `PS_D_QUANT_QUIZ` — Percentiles and quartiles: finding $Q_1$, $Q_2$, $Q_3$ from concrete ordered data, the interquartile range, the five-number summary, percentile rank, the 1.5*IQR outlier rule applied numerically, deciles, reading a quantile off a small data set.
- `PS_D_SPREAD_QUIZ` — Variance, standard deviation and z-scores: sample vs population variance and the $n-1$ divisor, computing variance and standard deviation of a small data set, range, coefficient of variation, z-score computations and interpretation, effect of linear transformations on the standard deviation, the empirical 68-95-99.7 rule.
- `PS_D_PLOTS_QUIZ` — Visual summaries: reading a histogram's shape and skew, box plots and what the whiskers show, stem-and-leaf plots, scatterplots, identifying the distribution shape from a described plot, choosing the right plot for a data type, spotting outliers on a box plot, bimodality.
- `PS_H_FRAMEWORK_QUIZ` — Hypotheses and significance: writing $H_0$ and $H_a$ for a described situation, one-tailed vs two-tailed, the significance level $\alpha$, the meaning of "reject $H_0$", test statistic construction, why $H_0$ is never "accepted", choosing $\alpha$ before seeing the data.
- `PS_H_TESTS_QUIZ` — Common one-sample tests: the z-test statistic $(\bar x-\mu_0)/(\sigma/\sqrt n)$ computed with numbers, the t-test and degrees of freedom $n-1$, when to use t instead of z, one-proportion z-tests, critical values for common $\alpha$, computing a test statistic from summary statistics, confidence-interval / test duality.
- `PS_H_PVAL_QUIZ` — p-values: the correct definition, computing or bounding a p-value from a z statistic, one-tailed vs two-tailed p-values (doubling), comparing p to $\alpha$, what a p-value is NOT (not the probability $H_0$ is true), effect of sample size on the p-value, borderline results.
- `PS_H_ERRORS_QUIZ` — Errors and power: Type I and Type II errors identified in described scenarios, $\alpha$ as the Type I rate and $\beta$ as the Type II rate, power $=1-\beta$, how sample size / effect size / $\alpha$ change power, the trade-off between the two error types, numeric power reasoning.
- `PS_R_CORR_QUIZ` — Correlation coefficient: the range of $r$, computing or interpreting $r$ for described scatterplots, $r$ being unitless and unchanged by linear rescaling, $r^2$ as the fraction of variance explained, $r$ near zero with a strong nonlinear relationship, sensitivity to outliers, the sign of $r$ matching the slope sign.
- `PS_R_ASSOC_QUIZ` — Association vocabulary: correlation versus causation, lurking and confounding variables, observational study versus randomized experiment, Simpson's paradox, extrapolation beyond the data range, ecological correlation, direction/form/strength language for describing a scatterplot.
- `PS_R_FIT_QUIZ` — The least-squares line: computing the slope $b=r\,s_y/s_x$ and intercept $a=\bar y-b\bar x$ from summary statistics, predicting $\hat y$ at a given $x$, the line always passing through $(\bar x,\bar y)$, interpreting the slope in context, minimizing the sum of squared residuals, effect of an influential point.
- `PS_R_RESID_QUIZ` — Residual diagnostics: computing a residual $y-\hat y$ numerically, residuals summing to zero for a least-squares fit, reading a residual plot for curvature or non-constant variance, high-leverage and influential points, the standard error of the estimate, when a linear model is inappropriate.
