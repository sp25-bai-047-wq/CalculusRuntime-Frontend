- `TAYLOR_CONCEPT_QUIZ` — the idea of polynomial approximation: matching value and derivatives at a center, first- and second-degree Taylor polynomials, why approximation degrades away from the center, comparing linearization to a quadratic approximation, reading a graph of $f$ against $T_n$.
- `TAYLOR_FORMULA_QUIZ` — the general Taylor formula $\sum f^{(n)}(a)(x-a)^n/n!$: identifying the center, computing individual coefficients $c_n=f^{(n)}(a)/n!$ for concrete functions, building $T_2$ or $T_3$ about a nonzero center such as $a=1$ or $a=\pi/2$, recovering $f^{(n)}(a)$ from a given coefficient.

- `TAYLOR_CATALOG_QUIZ` — manipulating known expansions: substitution ($e^{-x^2}$, $\sin(2x)$, $\cos(x^2)$), multiplying by a power of $x$, term-by-term differentiation and integration, combining two series, extracting a specific coefficient from a product.
- `TAYLOR_CONVERGENCE_QUIZ` — radius and interval of convergence: applying the ratio test to a power series, the radius $R$ for concrete series, testing endpoints, intervals for $\ln(1+x)$ and the geometric series, series with $R=0$ or $R=\infty$, effect of a shifted center on the interval.
- `TAYLOR_ERROR_QUIZ` — Taylor's inequality and remainder bounds: $|R_n(x)|\le M|x-a|^{n+1}/(n+1)!$, choosing $M$, bounding the error of a concrete approximation numerically, the alternating series error bound, choosing $n$ so the error is below a tolerance, Lagrange form of the remainder.
- `TAYLOR_ENGINEERING_QUIZ` — applied computational workflows: small-angle approximations, evaluating hard limits with series, approximating a definite integral such as $\int_0^1 e^{-x^2}dx$ with a few terms, relativistic/pendulum style linearizations described in words, and numerical evaluation of transcendental values.
- `TAYLOR_CHALLENGE_QUIZ` — harder mixed practice: composing two series, dividing series or finding the first terms of a quotient, using series to evaluate an indeterminate limit to a specified order, binomial series with fractional exponent, Euler's formula, and multi-step coefficient extraction.
